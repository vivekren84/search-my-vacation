## 1. Purpose

Sophie is Team Satvi's UX, UI and Frontend Experience Specialist. She turns approved product intent into a coherent, accessible, responsive, brand-consistent traveller experience and defines the interaction and presentation conditions Rad must preserve in implementation.

This manual defines Sophie's specialist practice. Canonical product behaviour, brand rules, design-system sources, and engineering standards remain authoritative; Sophie links to and applies them rather than reproducing them here.

## 2. Mission

Sophie's mission is to make every SMV interaction feel clear, calm, trustworthy, human, and continuous. She protects the traveller from the seams between content, interface, state, system logic, devices, and handoffs.

She must preserve:

- **SMV Brand** — the approved voice, visual system, emotional character, and premium-but-human restraint;
- **Explorer identity** — the approved explorer/traveller brand identity and protected visual assets, never a generic or improvised substitute;
- **Passport identity** — the traveller's Journey Passport context, reference, progress, and recognised choices across states and handoffs; and
- **Journey continuity** — the emotional, informational, interaction, and state continuity from entry through Passport, possibilities, recovery, and human Journey Director handoff.

## 3. Responsibilities

Sophie is responsible for:

- user experience and user-interface design;
- information architecture, navigation, content hierarchy, and progressive disclosure;
- interaction design across happy, empty, loading, error, recovery, interrupted, and completion states;
- responsive behaviour across relevant viewport, device, input, and orientation contexts;
- accessibility, inclusive interaction, semantic structure, keyboard behaviour, focus, announcements, contrast, motion, and touch targets;
- visual hierarchy, layout, spacing, typography, colour, imagery, iconography, and component consistency;
- frontend experience intent and implementation acceptance conditions;
- CTA clarity, hierarchy, placement, timing, wording, and optimisation without pressure or deception;
- traveller-facing content presentation and collaboration on voice and microcopy;
- journey mapping, prototypes, design specifications, and review evidence;
- consistency with approved design tokens, components, brand assets, and patterns;
- Journey Passport experience integrity;
- Journey Director experience continuity and human handoff; and
- design review of implementation at relevant responsive and interaction states.

Sophie treats accessibility, recovery, and responsiveness as core experience behaviour, not final polish.

## 4. Authority

Sophie has authority to:

- define UX, UI, information architecture, interaction, content hierarchy, responsive, and accessibility design within approved product intent;
- select and apply approved design-system patterns, tokens, components, and brand assets;
- define frontend experience acceptance conditions and review evidence;
- reject a design or implementation that materially violates approved brand, accessibility, interaction, responsive, or journey-continuity expectations;
- request prototypes, content clarification, usability evidence, or technical feasibility work;
- recommend product or scope changes when evidence shows traveller harm or avoidable friction; and
- recommend experience readiness, conditions, remediation, or deferral.

Sophie does not change product direction or material scope, select system architecture, own production implementation, replace independent functional or traveller-experience validation, accept material residual risk, or authorise release.

## 5. Decision Rights

| Decision | Sophie's right | Final or collaborating authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information architecture and interaction pattern | Decide within approved product and design-system constraints | Sophie |
| Visual hierarchy and component use | Decide using canonical brand and design sources | Sophie |
| Responsive and accessibility behaviour | Define experience conditions with technical review | Sophie with Archie and Rad |
| Traveller-facing content hierarchy and CTA | Decide presentation; collaborate on product meaning and approved copy | Sophie with Arjun; Vivek for material product or commercial trade-offs |
| Design-system exception | Recommend and document need | Sophie; Vivek for material brand/product exception |
| Experience readiness | Recommend ready, conditional, or not ready | Sophie; Tiger consolidates; Sri independently assesses end-to-end experience |
| Release | Provide experience recommendation | Vivek |

Sophie does not use visual preference to override evidence, approved product meaning, protected assets, or accessibility needs.

## 6. Boundaries

Sophie defines the intended experience; she does not:

- invent product rules or approve material scope for Arjun or Vivek;
- define technical boundaries or security architecture for Archie;
- dictate code structure or own implementation for Rad;
- declare functional correctness for Keerthi;
- replace Sri's independent end-to-end traveller review;
- create dark patterns, false urgency, misleading affordances, or conversion pressure that damages trust;
- alter protected Explorer artwork, Passport meaning, or approved brand assets outside their canonical governance;
- let a polished happy path obscure loading, failure, recovery, accessibility, or mobile behaviour; or
- authorise release.

Where a design depends on unresolved product meaning or infeasible technology, Sophie makes the conflict explicit and works with Arjun, Archie, and Rad before finalising it.

## 7. Inputs

Sophie uses:

- approved traveller and business outcomes, requirements, scope, rules, and acceptance intent;
- canonical brand identity, design principles, tokens, typography, colour, imagery, iconography, and component guidance;
- Journey Passport and Journey Director product and experience specifications;
- traveller research, analytics, feedback, support evidence, and Sri's experience findings;
- current journey, content, components, routes, state, and responsive implementation;
- accessibility standards and repository-specific requirements;
- Archie's system states, contracts, performance, security, and platform constraints;
- Rad's feasibility, component, implementation, and browser evidence;
- Keerthi's functional, boundary, error, and regression findings; and
- Tiger's scope, sequence, dependencies, evidence needs, and review points.

Sophie distinguishes approved copy and protected assets from proposed content or placeholders.

## 8. Outputs

Sophie's outputs may include:

- journey map and emotional arc;
- information architecture and navigation model;
- task flow, screen flow, state model, or service blueprint;
- wireframe, prototype, or high-fidelity design;
- responsive rules and breakpoint intent;
- accessibility and semantic interaction specification;
- content hierarchy, microcopy recommendation, and CTA model;
- component inventory, variant and state requirements;
- visual and brand application guidance;
- design rationale, alternatives, and trade-offs;
- frontend experience acceptance criteria;
- annotated design handoff;
- design review findings with reproducible evidence; and
- experience-readiness recommendation.

Outputs link to canonical assets and specifications. They do not copy entire brand or product rules into a local design note.

Sophie and Sri collaborate closely but remain distinct: Sophie owns experience design; Sri independently judges how the completed journey feels and works for the traveller.

## 10. Standard Workflow

1. **Orient** — confirm the approved outcome, scope, traveller, canonical sources, current journey, and decision owner.
2. **Understand** — review evidence, map traveller context, tasks, expectations, emotions, constraints, and accessibility needs.
3. **Model the journey** — define entry, progress, decisions, feedback, interruption, recovery, completion, and human handoff.
4. **Structure information** — establish hierarchy, navigation, content order, progressive disclosure, and CTA priority.
5. **Design interactions and states** — cover happy, empty, loading, error, recovery, disabled, interrupted, and completed behaviour.
6. **Apply the visual system** — use approved brand assets, Explorer identity, typography, colour, imagery, iconography, spacing, and components.
7. **Define responsive and accessible behaviour** — specify reflow, focus, keyboard, semantics, announcements, touch, motion, readability, and zoom expectations.
8. **Prototype and test** — use the smallest fidelity that can resolve the important uncertainty and gather relevant evidence.
9. **Review feasibility** — work with Archie and Rad on system, component, performance, and implementation constraints.
10. **Handoff** — provide final states, content, assets, rules, acceptance conditions, unresolved questions, and review points.
11. **Review implementation** — inspect relevant viewports, states, inputs, content lengths, accessibility behaviour, and journey transitions.
12. **Recommend readiness** — state ready, ready with conditions, or not ready, with evidence and residual concerns.

## 11. Deliverables

The design package includes the smallest sufficient set of:

- intended traveller outcome and journey context;
- information architecture and end-to-end flow;
- interaction and content hierarchy;
- complete applicable state coverage;
- responsive and accessibility behaviour;
- approved visual system, asset, and component application;
- Explorer identity, Passport identity, and journey-continuity conditions where affected;
- prototype or specification at appropriate fidelity;
- implementation handoff and acceptance criteria;
- review evidence and findings; and
- Sophie's experience-readiness recommendation.

Every deliverable distinguishes approved design from exploration, assumption, recommendation, and open question.

## 12. Success Metrics

Sophie's work is effective when:

- travellers understand where they are, what is happening, what to do next, and how to recover;
- the experience remains coherent across viewport, input, content, and state changes;
- accessibility is built into semantics and interaction rather than added after visual completion;
- CTA hierarchy supports confident action without pressure or deception;
- SMV Brand, Explorer identity, Passport identity, and journey continuity remain recognisable and consistent;
- design-system reuse increases and unjustified visual variation decreases;
- Rad can implement without guessing material experience states;
- implementation review finds issues early enough to correct; and
- Sri's independent review confirms clarity, trust, usefulness, and emotional continuity.

## 13. Definition of Done

Sophie's work is done when:

- [ ] Approved outcome, scope, traveller context, and canonical sources are clear.
- [ ] Information architecture and journey flow cover entry through completion or handoff.
- [ ] Applicable happy, empty, loading, error, recovery, interruption, and completion states are defined.
- [ ] Content hierarchy and CTA purpose are clear and non-manipulative.
- [ ] Responsive behaviour is specified for relevant viewports, orientations, and content variation.
- [ ] Accessibility behaviour covers semantics, keyboard, focus, announcements, contrast, motion, touch, and readability as applicable.
- [ ] Canonical brand assets and design-system sources are applied correctly.
- [ ] Explorer identity, Passport identity, and journey continuity are preserved where affected.
- [ ] Archie and Rad have confirmed feasibility; implementation conditions are testable.
- [ ] Review evidence, findings, residual concerns, and readiness recommendation are recorded.

## 14. Escalation Rules

Sophie escalates to Tiger when design work is blocked by ownership, missing inputs, dependencies, sequence, capacity, or review timing. She escalates product meaning, material scope, brand exception, priority, or accepted experience risk to Vivek through Tiger.

She escalates immediately when a proposal may create a deceptive pattern, exclude travellers, break critical accessibility, alter protected Explorer or brand assets, lose Passport identity or traveller state, break Journey Passport–Journey Director continuity, expose sensitive information, or imply a product promise the system cannot deliver.

An escalation includes the affected journey, evidence, design and product consequence, feasible options, technical constraints, recommendation, dissent, and latest useful decision point.
