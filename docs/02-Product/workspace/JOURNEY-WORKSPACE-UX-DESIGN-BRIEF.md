# 1. Purpose

This document provides the UX Design direction for Journey Workspace.

It communicates the Product Owner's vision, user experience expectations and design principles before UX Architecture and interaction design begin.

This document intentionally defines the desired user experience rather than interface design.

It should be treated as the primary design brief for the UX phase of Release 1.3.

---

# 2. Design Vision

Journey Workspace should feel like the operational heart of Search My Vacation.

It should provide Workspace Users with a calm, organised and unified environment where they can confidently manage traveller relationships without feeling overwhelmed.

The Workspace should reduce complexity rather than expose it.

Every interaction should reinforce confidence that travellers are receiving exceptional care.

---

# 3. Design Objective

The objective of Journey Workspace is not simply to manage travel enquiries.

Its purpose is to enable Workspace Users to confidently create exceptional traveller experiences.

Every design decision should help Workspace Users:

- understand what requires attention
- know what to do next
- complete work efficiently
- remain focused throughout the day
- feel confident that nothing important has been missed

---

# 4. Target Experience

Journey Workspace should create three distinct emotional moments.

## When logging in

Workspace Users should feel:

- organised
- informed
- inspired to begin their work

The Workspace should immediately communicate:

"I know exactly what is happening today."

---

## While working

Workspace Users should feel:

- focused
- productive
- supported
- in control

The Workspace should continuously answer:

"What should I do next?"

without requiring unnecessary searching.

---

## When logging out

Workspace Users should feel:

- satisfied
- relaxed
- confident that travellers are in good hands
- confident that nothing important has been missed

---

# 5. Workspace Personality

Journey Workspace should behave like an experienced, calm coworker.

It should:

- proactively surface important information
- organise work clearly
- reduce cognitive load
- highlight priorities
- avoid unnecessary interruptions
- support confident decision making

Journey Workspace should never feel noisy, cluttered or overwhelming.

---

# 6. UX Design Principles

## Journey First

The Journey is the primary operational object within the Workspace.

Navigation, workflows and information architecture should naturally revolve around active Journeys after traveller commitment.

---

## Inquiry First

Every new piece of work enters the Workspace as an Inquiry.

The Workspace should support the natural progression from Inquiry to Journey without forcing unnecessary steps.

---

## Progressive Disclosure

Present only the information required for the current task.

Allow users to progressively explore additional information when required.

Avoid information overload.

---

## Action Before Analytics

Prioritise actionable work over business reporting.

Workspace Users should immediately understand:

- what requires attention
- what is waiting
- what should happen next

Analytics should support decision making rather than dominate the experience.

---

## Calm by Design

Whitespace, hierarchy and visual simplicity should reduce cognitive load.

Every screen should help users focus rather than distract them.

---

## Consistency

Navigation patterns, terminology, layouts and interactions should remain consistent throughout the Workspace.

Users should not need to relearn common interactions.

---

# 7. UX Scope

The UX phase should define:

- Information Architecture
- Navigation Model
- User Journeys
- Screen Hierarchy
- Interaction Flows
- Screen Inventory
- Low-Fidelity Wireframes
- UX Design Standards
- Component Usage Guidelines

Visual design and implementation remain outside the scope of this phase.

---

# 8. Expected Deliverables

The UX phase should produce the following artefacts.

1. Workspace Information Architecture

2. Navigation Model

3. User Journey Maps

4. Screen Inventory

5. Interaction Flow Diagrams

6. Low-Fidelity Wireframes

7. UX Standards

8. UX Review Document

Each deliverable should maintain complete alignment with the Journey Workspace Business Lifecycle.

---

# 9. Design Constraints

The UX design shall:

- align with the approved Product Specification
- align with the Business Lifecycle
- preserve Product Owner terminology
- avoid introducing new business concepts
- avoid redefining business rules
- support future scalability
- remain desktop-first while considering responsive behaviour

---

# 10. Success Criteria

The UX phase will be considered successful if:

- Workspace Users can easily understand their priorities.
- Navigation feels intuitive and predictable.
- Workflows support the natural business lifecycle.
- Users spend minimal effort locating information.
- Every interaction reinforces confidence and control.
- The Workspace feels calm, organised and professional.
- The design clearly reflects Search My Vacation's relationship-first philosophy.

---

# 11. Handover to UX

This document represents the Product Owner's expectations for the Journey Workspace user experience.

The UX Designer is responsible for translating these principles into a complete UX Architecture and interaction model while preserving the business intent defined within the Journey Workspace Business Lifecycle.

UX decisions may improve usability but shall not alter the underlying business philosophy, lifecycle or business rules without Product Owner approval.
